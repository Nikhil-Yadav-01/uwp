# 3. Purchase → Warehouse Flow

Suppose you purchase:

> 100 units of Product A

### Step 1 — Purchase Order

```text
Purchase Order
PO-10001

Supplier: ABC Supplier

Product A
Qty: 100
Rate: ₹500
```

Status:

```text
Draft
 ↓
Approved
 ↓
Partially Received
 ↓
Fully Received
```

### Step 2 — Goods Receipt / GRN

Warehouse receives 100 units.

```text
GRN-10001

PO: PO-10001

Product A
Expected: 100
Received: 100
Damaged: 2
Accepted: 98
```

Inventory should **not automatically become available before your receiving/quality rules are satisfied**.

### Step 3 — Quality Check

```text
Received
   ↓
Quality Check
   ├── Accepted
   └── Rejected
```

### Step 4 — Put Away

The 98 accepted units are assigned to physical locations.

```text
Product A
98 Units

Warehouse: WH01
Zone: A
Rack: A01
Bin: A01-05
```

Now inventory becomes:

```text
Product A
WH01 / A01-05
Available Qty = 98
```

# 4. Inventory Structure

I recommend **not keeping inventory as only `Product.Quantity`**.

Instead:

```text
Product
   ↓
Inventory
   ↓
Warehouse
   ↓
Location/Bin
```

For example:

| Product | Warehouse | Bin    | Qty |
| ------- | --------- | ------ | --: |
| PROD001 | WH01      | A01-01 |  50 |
| PROD001 | WH01      | A01-02 |  30 |
| PROD001 | WH02      | B02-01 |  20 |

Total:

```text
PROD001 = 100
```

You can then calculate:

```text
Available
Reserved
Picked
Damaged
Blocked
InTransit
```

# 6. Picking Flow

The system should tell the warehouse employee exactly where to pick.

```text
Pick List PL-10001

Product A
Qty: 10
Location: WH01 / A01-05

Product B
Qty: 5
Location: WH01 / A02-03
```

Warehouse employee scans:

```text
Bin Barcode
     ↓
Product Barcode
     ↓
Quantity
     ↓
Confirm Pick
```

This is where barcode/QR scanning becomes extremely useful.

# 8. Dispatch

```text
Packed
   ↓
Dispatch Confirmation
   ↓
Stock Deduction
   ↓
Shipment Created
   ↓
Courier Handover
```

Depending on your business rules, **stock should generally be committed/deducted at a clearly defined point**, rather than simply when an order is created.

 ↓
Approval
 ↓

 ↓
In Transit
 ↓

 ↓

Example:

```text
WH01
Product A = 100

Transfer 30

WH01 = 70
In Transit = 30

Receive at WH02

WH02 = 30
```

# 10. Stock Adjustment

For damaged, missing, expired or manually corrected stock:

```text
Inventory
    ↓
Adjustment Request
    ↓
Reason
    ↓
Approval
    ↓
Stock Adjustment
```

Example:

```text
System Stock = 100
Physical Stock = 98

Difference = -2

Reason:
Damaged
```

Every adjustment should create an **audit/stock movement record**.

## 12. Very Important: InventoryTransactions

I strongly recommend maintaining a **stock ledger**.

For example:

```text
InventoryTransactions

TransactionId
ProductId
WarehouseId
LocationId
TransactionType
Quantity
ReferenceType
ReferenceId
CreatedBy
CreatedAt
```

Transaction types:

```text
PURCHASE_RECEIPT
PUTAWAY
SALE
PICK
TRANSFER_OUT
TRANSFER_IN
ADJUSTMENT_IN
ADJUSTMENT_OUT
RETURN
DAMAGE
```

Then you can answer:

> "Why did Product A decrease from 100 to 90?"

System can show:

```text
100  Purchase Receipt
 -5  Sales Order #1001
 -3  Sales Order #1002
 -2  Damaged
------------------
90   Current Stock
```

This is much safer than simply updating a quantity column with no history.
